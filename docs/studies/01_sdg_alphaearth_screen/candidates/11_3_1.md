# SDG 11.3.1 — Ratio of Land Consumption Rate to Population Growth Rate

**Custodian:** UN-Habitat (lead); UNSD; partners.
**Architecture letter:** **A** (AE-classification, annual area)
**Verdict:** **IN** (demonstrated case in `pipeline/main.py`).
**Status:** reference candidate — implementation already exists in this
repo for one country at a time.

## Indicator (post-2025-review text)

> Ratio of land consumption rate to population growth rate.

## Measurement target

The custodian methodology (UN-Habitat, *Metadata SDG 11.3.1*, current
release) defines the indicator as

```
LCRPGR = LCR / PGR
```

where `LCR` is the rate of change in built-up area between two years
(typically a 5-year reporting interval), and `PGR` is the rate of
change in urban population over the same interval, both expressed as
exponential annualised rates. The spatial unit is the **city** as
delimited by the *Degree of Urbanisation* (DEGURB) boundary. AOI is
typically a country, aggregated up from its cities.

**Ancillary non-EO data:** urban population. The methodology accepts
either national census + administrative urban boundaries or gridded
products derived from census redistribution (WorldPop GP, GPW). 11.3.1
remains in scope for Architecture A despite this dependency because
population enters as a denominator only; the classification itself is
entirely AE-based.

## Classification target

Two-class per-pixel: **built-up** vs **non-built-up**, classified
annually within the city boundary. *Built-up* follows the GHSL
operational definition: presence of any artificial impervious or roofed
structure. AE classification produces an annual binary built-up mask;
the difference between two years gives the change in built-up area
that feeds `LCR`.

## Architecture choice

**A is justified over C** here because:

- GHSL Built-S, the obvious C candidate, is at 100 m and reports
  fractional built-up surface that requires a thresholding choice.
  AE-classification at 10 m can reduce mixed-pixel error in
  fragmented peri-urban areas where 11.3.1 is most analytically
  important (low-density expansion is a primary policy concern).
- Annual cadence: GHSL is 5-year epochs and interpolates between them;
  AE provides genuine annual mosaics, allowing 11.3.1 to report at
  annual rather than 5-year intervals.
- Local label customisation: country-specific built-up phenotypes
  (informal expansion morphology, traditional dense low-rise) may be
  classified differently by GHSL's global thresholding than by a
  locally trained AE classifier.

## Recent literature (selected; full citations in `../literature.md`)

- Brown et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.22291) — AE Foundations paper: ~24%
  lower error rate vs. baselines on average; ~1.4× reduction in error
  magnitude across diverse mapping tasks.
- Burns & Kennedy 2026 (Google Earth Medium, 13 April) — for 11.3.1
  the appropriate regime is **annual-area, not change-map**: each year
  classified independently and aggregated; difference taken on areas,
  not on a change map. Burns & Kennedy's stacked / baseline-preserving
  features (configs 3–5) apply only if the deliverable is "where did
  built-up gain/lose ground" — useful for diagnostic mapping but not
  for the indicator value.
- *AlphaEarth Foundations: Implications for Cities and Urban Planners*
  (Urban AI on Medium, 2025/2026) — qualitative; useful framing.
- Geographic Data Service (UK) — *Using AlphaEarth Foundations
  Embeddings for Building Analysis* dataset — operational pilot for
  building-analysis use of AE.

## Validation strategy

- **Independent reference:** Hansen GFC `loss` and `gain` are not
  appropriate (they are forest-specific). Use GHSL Built-S epoch maps
  as one reference; cross-validate with WSF 2019 and ESA WorldCover
  2020/2021 for years where they overlap. For high-confidence ground
  reference, sample from Open Buildings Temporal V1 in regions where
  it has coverage.
- **Confusion classes:** bare ground in arid environments
  (false-positive built-up in hot deserts), tile-roofed traditional
  villages with vegetated courtyards (false negatives), greenhouses /
  agricultural structures (ambiguous in indicator definition — the
  custodian methodology is silent on this case).
- **Error stratification:** report per Köppen-Geiger climate zone or
  per GHSL SMOD class to surface systematic bias.

## Caveats

1. **Spatial-support mismatch (label vs target).** Most label products
   are at 30 m (Hansen) or 100 m (GHSL Built-S) — coarser than AE's
   10 m. Sampling labels at the coarser pixel and applying as the
   training class for the 10 m AE pixel is acceptable when the label
   product's class is dense within its pixel; mixed-pixel labels
   should be flagged or excluded from training.
2. **Label-provenance circularity.** Dynamic World, ESA WorldCover,
   and JRC GFC2020 are themselves Sentinel-2 reflectance derivatives.
   AE embeddings ingest Sentinel-2 among other modalities. Using
   these as labels for AE classification distils existing classifiers
   rather than providing independent supervision. GHSL Built-S
   (Sentinel-2 + Landsat) and WSF (Sentinel-1 SAR) have less or no
   circularity; prefer them.
3. **Population denominator.** WorldPop is itself modelled
   (RF dasymetric redistribution); biases at 100 m can be material
   in low-density urban fringe where 11.3.1 is sensitive.
4. **Custodian methodology accepts national census as primary**
   population source. Reporting LCRPGR with WorldPop denominator is
   defensible for inter-country comparison and for sub-national
   disaggregation but is a methodological substitution from the
   default.
5. **City boundary definition matters.** DEGURB / GHSL-SMOD vs national
   administrative boundary will give different LCRPGR values; report
   both where possible and label which boundary was used.

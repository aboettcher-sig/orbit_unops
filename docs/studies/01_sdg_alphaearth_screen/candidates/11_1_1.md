# SDG 11.1.1 — Urban Population Living in Slums, Informal Settlements or Inadequate Housing

**Custodian:** UN-Habitat.
**Architecture letter:** **A** (proxy) — see *Verdict context*.
**Verdict:** **IN with explicit proxy framing.**

## Indicator (post-2025-review text)

> Proportion of urban population living in slums, informal settlements
> or inadequate housing.

## Measurement target (custodian definition)

UN-Habitat defines "slum household" by **five household-level
deprivations**:

1. Access to improved water.
2. Access to improved sanitation.
3. Sufficient living area (no overcrowding).
4. Durable housing.
5. Security of tenure.

A household with one or more of these deprivations is a slum household.
The indicator is reported as proportion of urban population living in
such households.

This is fundamentally a **household survey** indicator. AOI is the
city or country; cadence is irregular (typically census or DHS-style
intervals).

**Ancillary non-EO data:** household survey microdata; population
denominator (WorldPop or census).

## Verdict context: why this is a *proxy* IN

The strict UN-Habitat definition cannot be EO-classified — household
deprivations are not visible from above. However:

- A substantial peer-reviewed literature maps **physical
  informal-settlement extent** as an EO-derivable proxy
  (Kohli et al. 2013; Mahabir et al. 2018; IDEAMAPS network 2021–2025;
  WSF deprived areas, DLR 2023).
- Many country statistical offices submit *EO-derived
  informal-settlement physical extent* to UN-Habitat as their
  reporting figure when household survey data are unavailable.
- AE classification of informal-settlement morphology — characterised
  by dense small-footprint roofs, irregular street geometry, and
  near-absence of vegetated open space — is a viable Architecture A
  task.

This candidate is therefore IN, with the explicit annotation that
**the indicator AE produces is a physical-extent proxy of the
custodian's household-deprivation indicator**, not the indicator
itself. Any reported number must carry this proxy framing.

## Classification target

Two-class per-pixel: **informal-settlement built-up** vs **other
built-up**, classified within an AE-derived or external general
built-up mask. The two-step pipeline is:

1. Classify built-up vs non-built-up (as in 11.3.1).
2. Within built-up pixels, sub-classify informal vs formal.

## Architecture choice

A with the explicit two-step structure:

1. AE-classification of built-up extent (already covered by 11.3.1
   pipeline).
2. AE-sub-classification within built-up pixels, using WSF Deprived
   Area + IDEAMAPS labels as supervision, distinguishing informal
   vs formal built-up by AE-encoded morphological signal.

C is not appropriate because no global discrete-class catalog product
of "informal settlement extent" exists at AE-overlapping years.

## Validation strategy

- **Reference:** IDEAMAPS pilot-city deprived-area polygons; WSF
  Deprived Area for the year(s) it covers; manual interpretation of
  high-resolution imagery (Maxar / Pleiades / Google).
- **Cross-product:** AE-classified informal vs WSF Deprived Area; AE
  vs IDEAMAPS in pilot cities.
- **Confusion classes:** dense traditional architecture (medina-style
  old quarters, kasbah morphology) is structurally similar to
  informal settlements at 10 m; legal-but-substandard apartment
  blocks; transitional peri-urban consolidation.
- **Stratification:** by region (Sub-Saharan Africa, MENA, S Asia, SE
  Asia, LAC) since informal-settlement morphology varies systematically.

## Caveats

1. **The proxy-vs-indicator gap is methodological, not technical.**
   Reporting EO-derived physical extent as if it were the
   household-deprivation indicator is a substitution that must be
   declared. UN-Habitat accepts EO physical extent in submissions but
   notes the methodological caveat.
2. **WSF Deprived Area coverage is uneven.** Validation in regions
   without WSF Deprived Area coverage relies on IDEAMAPS pilot data
   or manual interpretation; this limits global rollout.
3. **Definitional volatility.** "Slum" / "informal settlement" /
   "deprived area" are politically loaded terms; the EO-derivable
   morphological proxy is more defensible than any of them as a
   label, but the indicator as reported uses the UN-Habitat term.
4. **Spatial-support mismatch.** WSF Deprived Area is at 10 m so
   spatial support matches AE; IDEAMAPS polygons are vector boundaries
   that require rasterisation.
5. **Drift between informality and physical morphology.** Long-term
   informal-settlement consolidation (improved roofing materials,
   road paving, water and electricity) reduces morphological
   distinctiveness while household-level informality may persist.

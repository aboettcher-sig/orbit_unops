# SDG 15.1.1 — Forest Area as a Proportion of Total Land Area

**Custodian:** FAO.
**Architecture letter:** **A** (or **C**) — see "Architecture choice".
**Verdict:** **IN** with an explicit recommendation that **C** (direct
catalog aggregation) is the typical national-reporting practice and
**A** is justified only where local label customisation matters more
than catalog-default class schemas.

## Indicator (post-2025-review text)

> Forest area as a proportion of total land area.

## Measurement target

FAO's *Forest Resources Assessment* (FRA) defines forest as:

> Land spanning more than 0.5 hectares with trees higher than 5 metres
> and a canopy cover of more than 10 per cent, or trees able to reach
> these thresholds in situ. It does not include land that is
> predominantly under agricultural or urban land use.

The indicator is reported every five years (FRA cycle) but countries
can submit annual estimates; AE's annual cadence accommodates the
faster reporting variant. Spatial unit is the country (with possible
sub-national disaggregation).

**Ancillary non-EO data:** none for the EO component. National
forestry statistics may complement but do not feed the per-pixel
classification.

## Classification target

Two-class per-pixel: **forest** vs **non-forest**, where forest meets
the FAO definition (tree cover ≥ 10%, height ≥ 5 m, area ≥ 0.5 ha,
not under predominantly agricultural or urban use).

The "predominantly under agricultural or urban use" clause is the
methodologically loaded part: pure tree-canopy thresholds (Hansen
≥ 10% canopy) over-count agroforestry, oil-palm plantations, and
urban tree cover. JRC GFC2020 explicitly excludes "agricultural
plantations and urban areas" from its forest class — which is closer
to the FAO definition.

## Architecture choice

**The honest framing**: for the country-level FRA reporting use case,
**C (direct catalog aggregation)** is the appropriate primary
architecture today. Hansen GFC + JRC GFC2020 already provide
country-totals at acceptable accuracy, and their outputs are already
the basis for many countries' FRA submissions.

**A is justified when**:

- A country wants annual reporting (Hansen has annual loss but the
  baseline is 2000-fixed, requiring the analyst to derive annual
  forest extent indirectly; JRC GFC2020 is single-year). AE-based
  annual classification gives a directly comparable annual extent.
- The FAO "predominantly agricultural or urban use" exclusion needs
  country-specific calibration (e.g., distinguishing local
  agroforestry systems from forest).
- Sub-national or non-state-territory analysis where global product
  validation is weaker (e.g., contested or remote regions).
- The user wants to combine forest classification with damage,
  conflict, or other layers in a single AE-derived product — keeping
  the architecture homogeneous.

For 15.1.1 specifically in this project, **the recommendation is to
prototype both A and C and report side-by-side**, since the
discrepancy between AE-classification and Hansen-thresholded forest
is itself an informative finding for FRA reporting.

## Validation strategy

- **Reference:** FAO FRA national submissions (where available);
  high-resolution sampled photo-interpretation following the
  Olofsson et al. (2014) good-practice protocol.
- **Cross-product comparison:** AE-classified forest vs Hansen
  thresholded ≥ X% canopy vs JRC GFC2020 vs ESA WorldCover tree
  class vs PALSAR-2 FNF4 forest. Disagreements localise where the
  FAO definition's edge cases live.
- **Confusion classes:** plantation crops (oil palm, rubber, eucalyptus
  plantations classified as forest by canopy threshold), shrubland /
  woody savanna (height threshold near 5 m), urban tree canopy
  (excluded by FAO).
- **Stratification:** report per FAO eco-zone (boreal coniferous,
  boreal mountain, tropical moist, etc.) so systematic biases by
  forest type surface.

## Caveats

1. **Definitional alignment.** Hansen `treecover2000 ≥ 10%` is a *canopy
   threshold*, not the FAO forest definition. JRC GFC2020 V3 implements
   a closer FAO-aligned definition but is single-year. AE classification
   trained on either inherits its bias.
2. **Plantation accounting.** FAO FRA accepts planted forests as forest;
   the EU Deforestation Regulation explicitly excludes plantations.
   Reporting context determines which accounting is correct. AE
   classification can produce both with different label sets.
3. **Annual baseline drift.** Hansen has loss data but no per-year
   baseline forest extent published as a separate band; deriving
   annual extent requires "treecover2000 minus cumulative loss"
   approximation that ignores small-scale gain.
4. **Label-provenance circularity.** ESA WorldCover, JRC GFC2020, and
   Dynamic World are Sentinel-2 derivatives. AE includes Sentinel-2
   among inputs. Hansen (Landsat) and PALSAR-2 FNF4 (L-band SAR) are
   the most independent supervision sources.
5. **Pre-2017 baseline.** AE temporal coverage starts 2017. National
   FRA reporting often uses 1990 or 2000 baselines for forest-loss
   accounting. Pre-2017 baselines must come from non-AE products
   (Hansen 2000) and only the post-2017 trajectory uses AE.

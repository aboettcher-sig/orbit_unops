# SDG 6.6.1 — Change in the Extent of Water-Related Ecosystems Over Time

**Custodian:** UN Environment Programme (UNEP).
**Architecture letter:** **A** (with **C** as a viable alternative).
**Verdict:** **IN**.

## Indicator (post-2025-review text)

> Change in the extent of water-related ecosystems over time.

## Measurement target

UNEP's methodology splits the indicator into five sub-types of
water-related ecosystem, each measured separately and reported as
percentage change from a national baseline:

1. **Vegetated wetlands** (inland marsh, swamp, peat, etc.).
2. **Rivers and estuaries** — open water flow areas.
3. **Lakes** — permanent inland water bodies.
4. **Aquifers** (groundwater) — *not EO; submitted from national groundwater monitoring*.
5. **Artificial water bodies** (reservoirs).

For Architecture A, the four EO-mappable sub-types (1, 2, 3, 5) are
the targets. AOI is the country, with reporting at 5-year intervals
relative to a 2001–2005 baseline (per the latest custodian
methodology).

**Ancillary non-EO data:** none for the four EO-mappable sub-types.
Aquifer extent, the fifth sub-type, is reported separately by national
authorities and combined by UNEP in the published indicator value.

## Classification target

Per-pixel multi-class:

- Permanent open water (lakes, rivers, reservoirs)
- Seasonal open water (rivers, ephemeral lakes, paddies)
- Vegetated wetland (marsh, swamp, peat, mangrove)
- Non-water-related land (residual)

Mangroves are reported separately under SDG 6.6.1 only when the
country submits coastal wetland data; many countries report mangroves
under SDG 14 / 15 metadata. Inclusion here depends on country.

## Architecture choice

**A is justified over C** here primarily because:

- The custodian methodology requires multi-class classification
  (permanent vs seasonal water vs vegetated wetland), not just binary
  surface-water extent. JRC GSW alone gives the open-water classes
  but not vegetated wetland; ESA WorldCover gives all three but only
  for 2020/2021. AE can synthesise multi-year, multi-class output by
  ingesting both as labels.
- The vegetated-wetland class is the part that benefits most from AE.
  JRC GSW under-detects vegetated wetlands (they are not "water" in
  the GSW operational sense); ESA WorldCover detects them at single
  year only.

**C is appropriate** when the user only needs the open-water
sub-component (sub-types 2 and 3, plus 5 reservoirs). Then JRC GSW
Yearly History directly gives the time series.

## Recent literature (selected; full citations in `../literature.md`)

- **Streamlining Wetland Vegetation Mapping with AlphaEarth Embeddings:
  Comparable Accuracy to Traditional Methods with Cleaner Maps and
  Minimal Preprocessing** — *Remote Sensing* 18(2):293 (2025/2026)
  — directly relevant. The study compared a conventional multi-sensor
  RF classification with an AE-embeddings-derived RF classifier for
  the dynamic Narran Lake wetland (NSW, Australia) and found AE
  achieved comparable accuracy to the traditional workflow with
  smoother boundaries and reduced "salt-and-pepper" noise — and with
  most preprocessing steps eliminated. **This is the strongest
  benchmark we have for 6.6.1 specifically.**
- Brown et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.22291) — AE Foundations paper.
- Burns & Kennedy 2026 (Google Earth Medium) — for 6.6.1 the
  "extent of water-related ecosystems over time" is naturally an
  annual-area time series; A's annual classification + post-hoc
  area differencing is the right regime, not B's change-map regime.

## Validation strategy

- **Reference:** Ramsar Convention country wetland inventories;
  national wetland authority polygons; JRC GSW transitions for
  open-water cross-check.
- **Cross-product comparison:** AE-classified water vs JRC GSW Yearly
  History; AE-classified vegetated wetland vs ESA WorldCover class
  90; AE-classified mangrove vs GMW v3.
- **Confusion classes:** rice paddies (flooded vegetation; counted as
  wetland in some methodologies, agriculture in others — UNEP
  methodology excludes paddy from the wetland category); irrigation
  canals (artificial; sub-type 5); seasonally inundated grassland.
- **Stratification:** by Ramsar wetland type, by climate zone, and by
  whether the AOI year falls inside or outside an El Niño / La Niña
  phase, since seasonal wetland extent varies systematically with
  ENSO.

## Caveats

1. **Aquifer sub-type cannot be EO-mapped at AE resolution.** The
   indicator is reported partially from national groundwater data;
   the EO contribution is to the four surface sub-types only.
2. **Baseline period (2001–2005) precedes AE coverage.** AE annual
   classification can establish a 2017–2024 trajectory; the comparison
   to the 2001–2005 baseline must use Landsat-era products (JRC GSW
   for open water; legacy wetland inventories for vegetated wetland).
   This is a methodological splice and should be flagged in any
   indicator value reported.
3. **"Vegetated wetland" boundaries are notoriously fuzzy** —
   transition zones between marsh, fen, wet grassland, and seasonally
   inundated savanna admit no clean class boundary. Validation should
   report user's and producer's accuracy per sub-class, not just
   overall accuracy.
4. **Spatial-support mismatch.** JRC GSW labels are 30 m; AE pixels are
   10 m. Mixed-pixel labels at the 30 m grid should be excluded from
   training where the 30 m pixel does not have homogeneous class
   identity.
5. **GMW temporal gaps.** GMW v3 coverage is 1996, 2007–2010, then
   2015–2020 — gaps in the AE era. Mangrove labels may need to come
   from ESA WorldCover (single year) for years GMW does not cover.
